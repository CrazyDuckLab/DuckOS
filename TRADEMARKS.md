# DuckOS trademark and brand policy

This document explains how the **DuckOS** name, wordmark, rubber-duck
logo, and related product identity may be used by other people,
projects, and products.

This is a practical, plain-language policy. It is not legal advice.
If you have a specific question not covered here, open an issue.

## 1. Source code and trademarks are separate

DuckOS Community source is licensed to recipients under
**GPL-3.0-only**, as described in [`LICENSE`](LICENSE). That license
covers the source code and the text files in this repository.

GPL-3.0-only **does not** grant any right to use the DuckOS name,
wordmark, rubber-duck logo, or other product identity assets. Those
rights are separate and are described here.

For the broader context see [`COPYRIGHT.md`](COPYRIGHT.md) and
[`docs/licensing.md`](docs/licensing.md).

## 2. Marks covered by this policy

The following are DuckOS marks and product identity for the purposes
of this policy:

- the **DuckOS** name and any close variation that could be confused
  with it;
- the DuckOS wordmark as it appears in the project's official
  branding;
- the rubber-duck logo and any recognizable derivative of it;
- the favicon and other official product icons;
- product names and slogans used by the official project;
- the official brand layout, color palette, and other distinctive
  visual identity when used to identify a product as DuckOS.

This policy is **not** a registration document. Nothing in this
document should be read as a claim that any particular mark is a
formally registered trademark in any jurisdiction. Use of the `®`
glyph is reserved for marks that are actually registered; until then
no `®` appears in the project branding.

## 3. What you may do without asking

You may, without prior permission:

- refer to DuckOS by name in factual, descriptive text, including in
  articles, talks, posts, books, documentation, and academic work;
- use a phrase such as **"based on DuckOS"**, **"for DuckOS"**,
  **"DuckOS-compatible"**, **"compatible with DuckOS"**, **"runs on
  DuckOS"**, or **"uses DuckOS Community"** to describe a build,
  patch, integration, port, container, package, or service that
  actually is or works with DuckOS;
- link to the DuckOS project, releases, or documentation;
- cite DuckOS Community in changelogs, dependency lists, or
  acknowledgements;
- make your own original artwork, mascots, or wordmarks for your own
  product, provided they do not imitate the official DuckOS marks.

Factual references must stay accurate and not misleading.

## 4. What you may not do

You may **not**, without separate written permission from the
project owner:

- use the DuckOS name, wordmark, or rubber-duck logo (or close
  imitations) as the name or primary identity of your own fork,
  build, distribution, product, or service in a way that suggests
  it is an **official** DuckOS release, or that the project owner
  endorses, maintains, certifies, or supports it;
- use any DuckOS mark in a domain name, social media handle, package
  name, container name, app store listing, marketplace listing, or
  product listing in a way that suggests official status;
- use the rubber-duck logo as the icon or primary visual identity of
  a non-DuckOS product or service;
- use the DuckOS marks in marketing, advertising, or promotional
  material in a way that implies official endorsement of a third
  party, a third-party product, or a commercial offer;
- use the DuckOS marks in any manner that is deceptive, disparaging,
  or that could otherwise damage the reputation of the DuckOS
  project;
- combine the DuckOS marks with the marks of any other project,
  product, or company in a single combined logo or wordmark.

If you are unsure whether a specific use is "official-looking",
default to treating it as official-looking and choose different
wording or imagery.

## 5. Forks, builds, and community editions

If you publish a modified build of DuckOS, you may refer to it by
name as long as the name makes clear it is not the official DuckOS
release. Examples of acceptable naming:

- `AcmeOS (based on DuckOS Community vX.Y.Z)`
- `Acme Router (DuckOS-compatible fork)`

Examples of unacceptable naming:

- `DuckOS Pro`
- `Official DuckOS Distribution`
- `DuckOS by Acme`
- `The DuckOS Company Build`

A fork should also keep its own visual identity rather than copy the
official rubber-duck logo or wordmark.

## 6. Patches, integrations, and add-ons

If you publish a patch, integration, theme, plugin, or container that
targets DuckOS, you may describe it as **"for DuckOS"** or
**"DuckOS-compatible"**, and you may reference the DuckOS name in
metadata. Your own product name, icon, and wordmark should be
distinct.

## 7. Press, education, and research

Press coverage, conference talks, educational material, and academic
research may use the DuckOS name and may show the official
rubber-duck logo in truthful context (for example, in a slide that
identifies the project being discussed). They should not imply
endorsement of the third party by the project owner.

## 8. What to do if you need broader rights

If you want to do something that is not covered by section 3, open an
issue describing:

- what you want to use and on what;
- the audience and distribution channels;
- whether the use could be mistaken for an official DuckOS release;
- why the use is reasonable.

The project owner may grant additional permission at their
discretion. Permission, if granted, will be in writing and may be
limited in scope or duration.

## 9. No warranty

The marks are provided "as is". This policy does not grant any
warranty about non-infringement, registrability, or fitness for a
particular purpose. Compliance with trademark law in your
jurisdiction is your responsibility.

## 10. Reporting misuse

If you see a use of the DuckOS marks that you believe is misleading
or that violates this policy, open an issue or contact the project
owner through the channels listed in [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md).
For security-sensitive concerns, follow [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md).
